About

The short version

I am a Senior Research Fellow (SRF) in the Department of Physics at IIT Roorkee, working in the Centre for Photonics and Quantum Communication Technology with Prof. Sachin Kumar Srivastava. My subject is what light gives away about matter — specifically, the vanishingly small fraction of scattered photons that come back carrying a molecule's vibrational signature.

Most of my time goes to surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy: designing plasmonic substrates that amplify that signature by orders of magnitude, and then working out how much of the result is the sample and how much is the substrate. The current application is a receptor-free platform that identifies respiratory viruses from their intrinsic spectrum, with machine learning doing the separation that no single band can.

The rest goes to the things that make the first part usable. I wrote RNSpectraX because a reproducible analysis pipeline should not require three programs and two undocumented settings. I record Lab2Life because a result nobody outside the field can follow is only half finished.

What I work on

Four threads, one question

How little of a molecule can you detect, how fast, and how certain can you be about what it is? Each thread below attacks a different part of that question — the surface, the signal, the platform, and the analysis.

Surface

SERS biosensing without receptors

Raman scattering is famously weak — roughly one photon in a billion. Park a molecule nanometres from a plasmonic surface and that signal jumps by orders of magnitude. I build substrates that hold analytes in those hot spots reproducibly, so the enhancement is a measurement rather than an accident, and detect targets by their intrinsic fingerprint instead of a labelled receptor.

Signal

Machine learning on spectra

A biological SERS spectrum is a crowded superposition: hundreds of overlapping bands, a drifting fluorescence background, and sample-to-sample variation that dwarfs the difference you care about. Classifiers trained on properly preprocessed spectra separate classes that no single band distinguishes — provided the preprocessing is honest about what it removes.

Platform

Photonic and fibre-optic sensing

A spectrometer on an optical bench is not a diagnostic. Fibre Bragg gratings, waveguide couplers and integrated optical elements move a measurement from the bench toward something field-deployable, and each introduces its own trade-off between sensitivity, robustness and cost.

Analysis

Reproducible spectral software

Two labs applying "baseline correction" to the same spectrum can publish different peak ratios. RNSpectraX exposes every processing choice — despiking threshold, baseline order, smoothing window — so a pipeline can be reported precisely and reproduced exactly. It runs entirely in the browser, so no data leaves the machine that measured it.

Software

RNSpectraX

A full Raman and SERS analysis workspace that runs in a browser tab. Built because moving spectra between three pieces of proprietary software to make one figure is not a workflow — it is a reproducibility problem.

  • Reads your spectrometer. Ocean Optics, Horiba/LabSpec, Renishaw WiRE, Bruker, WITec, JCAMP-DX, binary SPC and plain two-column, detected automatically.
  • Cleans the signal properly. Cosmic-ray removal that leaves sharp bands intact, four baseline models including iterative ModPoly, Savitzky–Golay and three other filters — each with its parameters on screen.
  • Averages groups with real statistics. SD or SEM, as capped bars or shaded bands, computed across replicates you tag by name.
  • Finds what differs. The cross-file analyser clusters peaks across every loaded spectrum into common bands and sample-specific bands.
  • Plots and fits any XY data. A general scientific plotter with error bars and weighted least-squares fitting that reports every parameter with its standard error.
  • Keeps your data yours. Everything is computed in the tab. Nothing is uploaded, ever.

How it has gone so far

Research journey

2024

Into Raman and optical sensing

Started advanced work on Raman spectroscopy and optical sensing at the Centre for Photonics and Quantum Communication Technology, and began pairing spectral measurement with machine learning.

2025

A receptor-free SERS platform for respiratory viruses

Developed a machine-learning-integrated SERS platform for rapid respiratory virus detection — identifying targets from their intrinsic vibrational fingerprint rather than from a bound receptor, which removes the reagent that is usually the slowest and most fragile part of a diagnostic.

2026

RNSpectraX, and taking the science outward

Released RNSpectraX (Spectral Plotter and Analytics Suite), presented at the OPTCT + SeNcity conference at IIT Roorkee, and started publishing science communication — a podcast, explainers and lab footage — under Lab2Life.
