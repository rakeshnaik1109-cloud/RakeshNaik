A honeybee attack at Cautley Bhawan

Like every other morning I was walking back from the gym, already thinking about breakfast, when something unusual caught my attention. A boy was running across the courtyard, frantically brushing at his head.

Then I heard it — a sharp, unsettling buzz behind me. Within seconds I realised a few bees were following me. My heart started pounding. I did not look back. I simply ran as fast as I could.

When I finally reached a safer distance, some students noticed and helped shoo away the bees still clinging to my jacket. That moment of relief felt priceless. I hurried back to my room, trying to process what had happened, and then it hit me: I had left my keys in the gym.

Nature has its own harsh arithmetic. A honeybee can sting once. The barbed sting tears free of her abdomen as she pulls away, and she dies for it. Every sting is a colony spending one life to make the intruder leave — a defence that only makes sense because the colony, not the individual, is the thing being defended.

I have thought about that more than once since, sitting in front of an instrument at two in the morning. Some systems are built so that the part sacrifices itself for the whole. It is a strange thing to admire while running away from it.
